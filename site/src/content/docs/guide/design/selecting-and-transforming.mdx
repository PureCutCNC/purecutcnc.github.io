Most editing starts with a selection. Once features are selected, the transform tools appear in
the tool rail, and the same commands are in the context menu. Transforms change where a feature
is, not its shape definition, so they affect only the selected copies of a linked feature; see
[Linked features](/guide/fundamentals/linked-features/).

## Before you start

- Open the **Sketch** view.
- Unlock the features you want to change. While a locked feature is selected, the transform tools
  are unavailable.

## Select features


<VisualPending id="selection-overlap-picker" />
| To select | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| One feature | Click its outline or inside it, or click its row in the feature tree. |
| More features | <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+click (on macOS, <kbd>Cmd</kbd>+click). Clicking a selected feature this way removes it from the selection. On a tablet, turn on **Multi** and tap. |
| Features in an area | Drag a box on empty canvas. Every visible feature that lies completely inside the box is added to the selection. |
| All visible features | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd> (<kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd>). |
| A whole folder | **Select all features in folder** on the folder's row. |
| Nothing | Click empty canvas. |

Clicking a member of a group selects the whole group. See
[Feature tree and properties](/guide/fundamentals/feature-tree/#folders-and-groups).

When shapes overlap, a click inside them selects the innermost shape, the one completely enclosed by
the others, or else the topmost. When you click where two or more outlines lie on top of each other,
the **Select feature** panel lists them (**N features overlap here. Choose one to select.**);
choose the one you mean.

## Transforms


<VisualPending id="selection-transform-panel" />
The transform tools work on the selected features. Each opens a workflow panel on the canvas;
**Cancel** or <kbd>Esc</kbd> ends it without changing anything, and the tool's button in the rail
cancels it too.

### Move

<Steps>

1. Click **Move selected features**.

2. At **Select from point**, click a reference point, for example a corner of the feature.

3. At **Select target point**, click where that point should go. The features move by the
   difference.

</Steps>

To move by an exact distance, press <kbd>Tab</kbd> after the first point: point in the direction to
move, type the **Distance**, and click **Confirm**.

### Copy

Copy works like move, but leaves the originals in place.

<Steps>

1. Click **Copy selected features**.

2. Click the reference point, then the target point, or press <kbd>Tab</kbd> to type the
   **Distance**.

3. At **Set copy count**, enter the number of **Copies** and click **Confirm**. With 3 copies, the
   copies are placed one, two, and three times the distance from the original.

</Steps>

Copies are linked to the original. Copying a whole group creates a new group.

### Resize

Resize scales along a reference line that you draw.

<Steps>

1. Click **Resize selected features**.

2. At **Select first reference**, click the point that stays fixed.

3. At **Select second reference**, click a second point. The distance between the two points is the
   original size.

4. At **Scale to commit**, move the pointer along the reference line to preview, and click to
   finish. Or click **Scale** (<kbd>Tab</kbd>) and type a factor, such as 2 to double the size.

</Steps>

The direction of the reference line decides what is scaled:

- a line within 12° of a feature's width or height direction scales only that direction;
- any other line scales both directions, each by its own ratio;
- imported models always scale evenly, including their height.

Draw the reference line along the dimension you want to change, and use a diagonal line only when
you mean to scale both.

### Rotate

<Steps>

1. Click **Rotate selected features**.

2. At **Select origin**, click the centre of rotation.

3. At **Select reference direction**, click a point that sets the starting angle.

4. At **Rotate to commit**, move the pointer to preview and click to finish. Or click **Angle**
   (<kbd>Tab</kbd>) and type the angle in degrees.

</Steps>

Turn on **Keep originals** (<kbd>K</kbd>) to rotate copies instead: after you click to set the
angle, enter the number of **Copies**. Each copy is rotated one more step, so 5 copies at 60° fill
the rest of a circle around the original.

### Mirror

<Steps>

1. Click **Mirror selected features**.

2. At **Select first line point** and **Select second line point**, click two points on the mirror
   line. The preview follows the pointer until the second click.

</Steps>

With **Keep originals** (<kbd>K</kbd>) on, the mirrored features are copies and the originals stay.
Construction geometry makes a good mirror line: snap to it; see
[Construction geometry](/guide/fundamentals/construction-geometry/).

### Delete

**Delete selected features** removes the selection. **Undo** brings it back.

<Aside type="caution" title="Transforms move operation targets too">
	Operations follow the features they machine. After moving, resizing, or rotating a feature,
	check that its toolpaths still stay inside the stock and clear of clamps.
</Aside>

## Clipboard

The clipboard copies whole features: their shape, role, Z range, and properties.

| Shortcut | Action |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> | Copy the selected visible features. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>X</kbd> | Copy the selected features and remove them. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> | Paste. |

After you paste, the **Paste features** panel asks you to **Click in the sketch to place** the
features. They follow the pointer, keeping their arrangement, and snapping applies to the click.

The clipboard belongs to PureCut CNC, not to the system: you cannot paste features into another
program. It is kept while you open or create another project in the same window, so you can copy
features from one project into another. On macOS use <kbd>Cmd</kbd>; the desktop app's **Edit**
menu has the same commands.
