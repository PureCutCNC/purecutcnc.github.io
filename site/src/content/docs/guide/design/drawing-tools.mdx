The drawing tools in the tool rail add shapes to the **Sketch** canvas. Every shape you draw becomes
a feature in the feature tree; its role and Z range decide what it does in the model, as
[Features, roles, and depth](/guide/fundamentals/features-and-roles/) explains. This page covers
the basic shapes. Slots, regular polygons, gears, and rectangles with shaped corners are on
[Slots, polygons, and gears](/guide/design/parametric-shapes/), and text is on
[Text](/guide/design/text/).

## Before you start

- Open the **Sketch** view.
- Snapping is on by default, so points you click snap to the grid and to existing geometry. See
  [Snapping and the grid](/guide/design/snapping-and-grid/).

## Draw a shape

<Steps>

1. Choose a creation target at the top of the tool rail (see below).

2. Open the shape drawer and choose a shape. See [Shape drawer](#shape-drawer) below.

3. Follow the step shown in the shape's workflow panel: click points on the canvas, or click
   **Dimensions** (or press <kbd>Tab</kbd>) to type exact values.

4. When the shape is complete, the tool re-arms for another shape of the same kind, with the same
   settings. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> or click **Cancel** when you are done.

</Steps>

Tools stay armed so that you can place a row of circles or a set of slots without returning to the
tool rail. Per-shape settings such as a polygon's side count or a rounded rectangle's corner radius
carry over to the next shape.

## Shape drawer

The tool rail's <AppIcon name="feature-drawer" /> **Choose feature shape** button opens the shape
drawer. Its top row holds the shapes on this page — rectangle, circle, ellipse, polygon, spline,
and composite — together with text; its second row holds the parametric shapes: slot, regular
polygon, gear, rounded rectangle, and chamfered rectangle. Each button's tooltip names the creation
target and the shape, for example **Add feature rectangle**.

The button directly below the drawer button repeats the last shape you used, so you can re-arm that
shape without opening the drawer again.

<Screenshot src={shapeDrawer} alt="Tool rail with the shape drawer open, showing rectangle, circle, ellipse, polygon, spline, composite, and text in the top row, and slot, regular polygon, gear, rounded rectangle, and chamfered rectangle in the second" maxWidth={262} />

## Creation target

The four buttons at the top of the tool rail decide what the next shape becomes. The drawing
tools are the same for every target.

| Button | New shapes become |
| --- | --- |
| **Create features** | Closed shapes become add or subtract features; open paths become line features. This is the default. |
| **Create lines** | Line features, open or closed, for engraving and V-carving. |
| **Create regions** | Region masks that limit where operations cut. Regions must be closed. |
| **Create construction geometry** | Construction geometry for reference only. It is never machined. |

A status label on the canvas shows the current target, for example **Drawing features**.

With **Create features**, PureCutCNC chooses add or subtract for each closed shape: the first
solid in a project is always an add feature, and a shape drawn inside an add feature becomes a
subtract feature. Change the role afterwards in the feature's properties if you need to. See
[Regions](/guide/fundamentals/regions/) and [Construction
geometry](/guide/fundamentals/construction-geometry/) for what those targets are for.

<Screenshot src={creationTarget} alt="The four creation target buttons at the top of the tool rail: features, lines, regions, and construction geometry" maxWidth={80} />


## Typed dimensions

Every drawing tool takes typed values instead of a second click. Press <kbd>Tab</kbd>, or click the
panel's typed-entry button, and the workflow panel becomes a small form. The button names what it
will ask for: **Dimensions (Tab)** on the basic shapes, **Radius (Tab)** on a gear or regular
polygon, **Width (Tab)** at a slot's width step.

The pending shape follows the numbers as you type them, so you can check the size before you
confirm. Confirm places the shape; cancel returns you to clicking.

<Screenshot src={typedDimensions} alt="Rectangle workflow panel in its Enter dimensions form with Width 2.5 and Height 1.5, and the pending rectangle on the canvas reading 2.5 by 1.5 to match" />

## Rectangle

<Steps>

1. Choose the <AppIcon name="rect" /> rectangle in the shape drawer.

2. At **Click first corner**, click one corner.

3. At **Click opposite corner or enter dimensions**, click the opposite corner, or click
   **Dimensions** and enter the **Width** and **Height**.

</Steps>

Rectangles are drawn aligned with the X and Y axes. Rotate one afterwards with the rotate tool; see
[Selecting and transforming](/guide/design/selecting-and-transforming/).

<Screenshot src={rectangleTool} alt="A pending rectangle on the canvas with width and height readouts, and the Rectangle workflow panel asking for the opposite corner or dimensions" />


## Circle

<Steps>

1. Choose the <AppIcon name="circle" /> circle in the shape drawer.

2. At **Click center point**, click the centre.

3. At **Click to set radius or enter dimensions**, click a point on the circle, or click
   **Dimensions** and enter the **Radius**.

</Steps>

Circles are stored as true circles, not as polygons, so pocket, edge route, and drill operations
follow the exact curve. The drill operation accepts only circle features.

<Screenshot src={circleTool} alt="A pending circle with its radius readout, and the Circle workflow panel asking for the radius or dimensions" />


## Ellipse

<Steps>

1. Choose the <AppIcon name="ellipse" /> ellipse in the shape drawer.

2. At **Click center point**, click the centre.

3. At **Click to set radii or enter dimensions**, click a point to set both radii, or click
   **Dimensions** and enter the ellipse's **Width** and **Height**.

</Steps>

Ellipses are stored as true curves, and toolpaths follow the curve.

<Screenshot src={ellipseTool} alt="A pending ellipse drawn from its centre with its radius readout repeated in each quadrant, and the Ellipse workflow panel asking for the radii or dimensions" />


## Polygon

A polygon is a path of straight segments through the points you click.

<Steps>

1. Choose the <AppIcon name="polygon" /> polygon in the shape drawer.

2. At **Click first point**, click the first point, then keep clicking to add points
   (**Add one more point**, then **Add points or close**).

3. Finish the shape:

   - click the first point again to close it;
   - or click **Finish** (or press <kbd>Enter</kbd>) to keep it as an open path.

</Steps>

While you draw, **Undo** (<kbd>Backspace</kbd>) removes the last point, and **Dimensions**
(<kbd>Tab</kbd>) sets the next segment's **Length** and **Angle**. **Finish** needs at least two
points, and it is not offered when drawing regions, which must be closed.

A closed polygon can be pocketed, routed, V-carved, or surface-cleaned. An open path becomes a
line feature that the engrave operation can follow.

<Screenshot src={polygonTool} alt="A pending polygon with segment length readouts, and the Polygon workflow panel with Finish, Dimensions, Undo, and Cancel" />


## Spline

A <AppIcon name="spline" /> spline is a smooth curve that passes through every point you click. Draw it like a polygon:
click the first point again to close it, or click **Finish** (<kbd>Enter</kbd>) to keep it open.
**Undo** (<kbd>Backspace</kbd>) removes the last point.

To change the curve afterwards, edit it in a sketch-editing session; see
[Editing sketches](/guide/design/sketch-editing/).

<Screenshot src={splineTool} alt="An unfinished spline curving through four points, and the Spline workflow panel with Finish, Dimensions, Undo, and Cancel" />


## Composite

A composite path mixes straight lines, arcs, and spline curves in one feature.

<Steps>

1. Choose the <AppIcon name="composite" /> composite shape in the shape drawer, then click the first
   point.

2. Choose the kind of the next segment with the panel's **Line** (<kbd>L</kbd>), **Arc**
   (<kbd>A</kbd>), and **Spline** (<kbd>S</kbd>) buttons. The step reads **Add line points**,
   **Add arc points**, or **Add spline points**.

3. Click to add segments. An arc takes two clicks: its end point, then a point that sets its
   curvature (**Click arc curvature point**).

4. Click the first point to close the path, or click **Finish** (<kbd>Enter</kbd>) to keep it
   open. **Finish** needs at least one segment.

</Steps>

**Undo** (<kbd>Backspace</kbd>) removes the last segment. **Dimensions** (<kbd>Tab</kbd>) sets the
next segment's **Length** and **Angle**, and for an arc also its **Radius**.

<Screenshot src={compositeTool} alt="A composite path in progress with a straight segment, an arc, and a spline, and the Composite workflow panel with Line, Arc, and Spline mode buttons" />


<Aside title="Open and closed paths">
	Closed profiles can be pocketed, edge-routed, V-carved, and surface-cleaned. Open paths are line
	features: the engrave operation is the one that machines them.
</Aside>
