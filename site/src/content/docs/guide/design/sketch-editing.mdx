A sketch-editing session changes the points and segments of one feature. The session collects your
edits; **Finish editing** keeps them and **Cancel editing** throws them away.

## Before you start

- Text cannot be edited this way: **Edit sketch** is unavailable for text features. Expand the text
  first if you need to reshape letters; see [Text](/guide/design/text/#expand-text).
- The feature must not be locked.
- Editing the shape of a linked feature changes every linked copy. Make the copy unique first if
  only this one should change; see [Linked features](/guide/fundamentals/linked-features/).

## Edit a sketch


<VisualPending id="sketch-edit-session" />
<Steps>

1. Double-click the feature on the canvas, or select it and click **Edit sketch** in its properties
   or context menu. The **Edit sketch** panel opens, and the feature's points are shown.

2. Make your changes. With **Move** active (the default), the step reads **Drag nodes, or click a
   segment to edit its dimensions**:

   - drag a point, or a curve handle, to move it;
   - click a segment to show its **Selected segment** fields, **Length** and **Angle**, plus
     **Radius** for an arc. Type to update the segment as you go.

3. Pick a tool from the panel's row for other edits (see below).

4. Click **Finish editing**, or press <kbd>Enter</kbd> when no tool is active. To discard all edits,
   click **Cancel editing** or press <kbd>Esc</kbd> when no tool is active.

</Steps>

<kbd>Tab</kbd> starts dimension entry for the segment under the pointer. <kbd>Alt</kbd> locks point
dragging to the X or Y axis; see [Snapping and the grid](/guide/design/snapping-and-grid/#axis-lock).
<kbd>C</kbd> starts **Add constraint** for the feature.

The panel warns while you edit if the profile crosses itself (**Self-intersecting profile**) or
reaches outside the stock (**Extends outside stock**). You can still finish, but toolpaths and the
3D model may be wrong for a self-intersecting profile.

## Editing tools


<VisualPending id="sketch-edit-tools" />
Choose a tool in the **Edit sketch** panel. The step line says what to click. A tool stays active
until you choose **Move** or another tool; <kbd>Esc</kbd> or <kbd>Enter</kbd> leaves it and keeps
what you did with it.

| Tool | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Add point** | Click a segment to insert a point there, splitting the segment in two. |
| **Delete point** | Click points to remove them. The segments on either side are joined. |
| **Delete segment** | Click segments to remove them. Deleting a segment of a closed profile opens it. Deleting one from the middle of an open path splits it into two features; the second is named **Split**. |
| **Disconnect point** | Click a point to split the profile there (**Click an anchor to split**). A closed profile opens at that point; an open path is split into two features. |
| **Round corner / fillet** | Click a corner, then click a second point or click **Radius** to type the radius. The corner becomes an arc. |
| **Chamfer corner** | Click a corner, then click a second point or click **Distance** to type it. The corner is cut off by a straight line, the same distance along both sides. |
| **Trim to cutting edge** | Click the part of a segment to remove, then click the segment that cuts it. |
| **Extend to target** | Click near the open end of a segment, then click the segment it should reach. |

Some tools only work on some geometry:

- Fillet and chamfer need a corner between two straight segments.
- Trim and extend work on open paths only; while you edit a closed profile their buttons are
  unavailable.
- A circle cannot be split or have a segment deleted.

With trim and extend, the first <kbd>Esc</kbd> clears a segment you have picked without leaving
the tool.

<Aside type="caution" title="Opening a profile changes what can machine it">
	Deleting a segment or disconnecting a point of a closed profile leaves an open path. Open paths
	can only be engraved: pockets, edge routes, and V-carves that used the feature can no longer
	machine it. Check the feature's operations after editing.
</Aside>

## Join open paths

To connect two open paths, drag an endpoint of the path you are editing onto an endpoint of another
open path. The other path becomes part of this one. Dragging one end of a path onto its own other
end closes it.
