PureCut CNC has three ways to work with sizes on the sketch:

- the **tape measure** reads a distance and leaves nothing behind;
- **dimensions** are labels saved with the project that follow the geometry, and some of them can
  resize it;
- **constraints** keep a feature at a fixed distance from another point.

Dimensions and the tape measure never affect machining. Constraints move features, and so the
toolpaths of those features.

## Measuring tools

<span id="toolbar"></span>

The top toolbar holds **Tape measure**, **Add dimension** (a menu of six types), **Delete
dimension**, and **Show/hide dimensions**. On a tablet they are in the measure popover.

| Dimension | Measures |
| --- | --- |
| **Aligned dimension** | The straight distance between two points. |
| **Horizontal dimension** | The X distance between two points. |
| **Vertical dimension** | The Y distance between two points. |
| **Radius dimension** | The radius of a circle or arc. |
| **Diameter dimension** | The diameter of a circle or arc. |
| **Angle dimension** | The angle between two lines that meet at a point. |

## Tape measure

<span id="tape"></span>

<Steps>

1. Click **Tape measure**.

2. At **Click the first point** and **Click the second point**, click two points. Snapping works as it
   does when drawing.

3. Read the distance, the X and Y distances (Δx, Δy), and the angle next to the measurement.

4. Click again to start a new measurement. Click **Done** or press <kbd>Esc</kbd> when you have
   finished.

</Steps>

The measurement follows the pointer until the second click, then stays until your next click. Tape
measurements are not saved and are not part of undo.

## Placing dimensions

<span id="placing"></span>

<Steps>

1. Open **Add dimension** and choose a type.

2. Click the points the panel asks for:

   | Type | Clicks |
   | --- | --- |
   | Aligned, horizontal, vertical | **Click the first point**, **Click the second point**, then **Click to set the offset** to place the dimension line. |
   | Radius, diameter | **Click the circle / arc center**, then **Click a point on the edge**. |
   | Angle | **Click the vertex**, **Click the first ray point**, **Click the second ray point**, then **Click to place**. |

3. The dimension appears and is saved with the project. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> at any step to cancel.

</Steps>

Each click snaps to a feature corner, midpoint, centre, or edge, to the stock, or to the origin. The
dimension remembers what it is attached to, not the position: when you move or edit the geometry,
its value, lines, and label update.

If a feature a dimension is attached to is deleted, the dimension stays in the project and is marked
with a warning sign where it was attached, until you delete it.

## Editing dimensions

<span id="driving"></span>

- **Select** a dimension by clicking its line or label. Drag an aligned, horizontal, vertical, or
  angle dimension to move its line; radius and diameter labels stay where they are.
- **Delete** a selected dimension with <kbd>Delete</kbd> or <kbd>Backspace</kbd>. To delete several,
  click **Delete dimension** (**Delete dimension (click one)**) and click each one; click **Done** or
  press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to stop.
- **Drive** a size: click the label of a dimension that can change the geometry. The **Edit
  dimension** panel opens.

<Steps>

1. At **Set value**, type the new value.

2. Check which end stays in place. The panel says which point is held; **Flip held point** holds the
   other end instead.

3. Click **Apply**. The feature is resized to the new value.

</Steps>

A dimension can drive the geometry when:

- both its points are on the same feature and the moving point is a corner you could drag; or
- it is a radius or diameter of a circle or arc; or
- it is the width or height label of rectangular stock (this opens **Resize stock**).

Other dimensions only measure. A dimension also cannot drive while it is hidden or while the feature
it measures is missing.

<Aside type="caution" title="Driving a dimension changes linked copies">
	Driving a dimension edits the feature's shape, which linked copies share. Make the copy unique
	first if only one should change. See [Linked features](/guide/fundamentals/linked-features/).
</Aside>

## Visibility

**Show/hide dimensions** shows or hides every dimension at once; its tooltip counts them (for example
**Hide dimensions (3)**). Hidden dimensions cannot be clicked, so clicks reach whatever is under them.
The setting is saved with the project. The tape measure is not affected.

## Limits

- Dimensions appear in the Sketch view, and in printed designs and exported SVG files while they are
  shown. They are not in the 3D view, the simulation, or the G-code.
- Dimensions attach only to features, the stock, and the origin. They do not follow tabs or clamps.
- To set a size exactly without a drivable dimension, use the feature's properties, the drawing
  tools' **Dimensions** entry, or a sketch-editing session.

## Constraints

A constraint keeps a point on a feature at a fixed distance from another point. PureCut CNC moves the
feature to keep that distance, and moves it again when the other point moves.

<Steps>

1. Select the feature to position.

2. Click **Add constraint** in the tool rail, or choose **Add constraint** in the context menu. In a
   sketch-editing session, press <kbd>C</kbd>.

3. At **Pick anchor point**, click a snap point on the selected feature (**Tap a snap point on this
   feature.**).

4. At **Pick reference point**, click a snap point on another feature (**Tap a snap point on another
   feature.**).

5. At **Set distance**, type the distance and click **Confirm**.

</Steps>

The feature moves along the line between the two points until the distance matches.

The constraint is listed under **Constraints** in the feature's properties, with its value, what it
refers to, and a short type:

| Type | Distance to |
| --- | --- |
| **point** | a corner of the other feature |
| **center** | the centre of the other feature |
| **midpt** | the midpoint of a segment |
| **line** | a point part-way along a segment (the tooltip gives the percentage) |
| **perp** | the nearest point on a segment, measured at a right angle |
| **intersect** | the point where two segments cross |

A reference that is not on a feature is shown as **World**. To change a constraint's value, click
its label on the canvas and type the new distance in the **Edit constraint** panel. The **×** button
next to a constraint (**Delete constraint**) removes it.

A constraint that cannot be met, for example because its reference feature was deleted, is marked
with a warning; hover over it to see why.
