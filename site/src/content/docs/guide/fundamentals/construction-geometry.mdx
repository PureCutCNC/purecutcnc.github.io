Construction geometry is reference geometry for drawing: centrelines, symmetry axes, bolt circles,
and layout guides. You can snap to it, mirror against it, and dimension to it, but it never
becomes part of the part.

## Main idea


<Screenshot
	src={constructionGeometry}
	alt="Project tree with a Construction section containing a centre line marked REF, and the canvas showing that line drawn dashed across a green add feature and blue subtract features"
	caption="Construction geometry is drawn dashed, carries a ref badge, and is never machined."
/>
- **Create** construction geometry with the **Create construction geometry** target in the tool
  rail. Any shape can be construction geometry, open or closed. New items are named
  **Construction** with a number.
- **Convert** an existing feature by changing its **Operation** to **Construction** in its
  properties, or in the role menu on its tree row.
- **Find** it in the **Construction** section of the feature tree, marked with a **ref** badge. It
  has its own folders and can only be grouped with other construction geometry.

Construction geometry:

- adds no material to the 3D model and has no Z range (**Not machined**);
- cannot be an operation target: the Add menu says **Construction geometry is never machined —
  deselect construction features first**;
- is not simulated and is left out of exported models;
- is drawn as dashed reference lines on the canvas and in printed designs.

## Typical uses

- Draw a centreline or symmetry axis first, then mirror the finished features across it.
- Draw a circle as a bolt circle, then snap hole centres to it or use it as the guide for
  **Feature distribution** along a path.
- Trace the outline of an imported drawing as a reference while you draw the real features
  separately.
- Dimension from construction geometry to the features that depend on it.

To tidy the view once the design is done, hide construction geometry with **Hide all construction
geometry** on its tree row, or with the **Construction** toggle in the status bar. Hiding it
changes nothing in the model or the toolpaths.
