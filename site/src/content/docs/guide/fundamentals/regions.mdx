A region is a closed shape that limits where an operation may cut. The operation's targets still
say what to machine; the region says where. A region is never material, never a cut on its own,
and never turned into a toolpath along its edge.

## Main idea

- **Create** a region with the **Create regions** target in the tool rail, or change a closed
  feature's **Operation** to **Region mask**. Regions must be closed. They are listed in the
  **Regions** section of the feature tree and named **Region** with a number.
- **Set its mode** with **Mask mode** in its properties: **Include** (the default) or **Exclude**.
  The tree row shows an **include** or **exclude** badge.
- **Apply it** by selecting it together with the targets when you add an operation, or by
  right-clicking it and choosing **Add to operation**. An operation ignores regions it was not
  given. The operation's target then lists the regions as filters.

A region's Z range always follows the stock (**Follows stock**): it filters in XY through the
whole thickness.

## Include and exclude

- An **include** region allows cutting inside it.
- An **exclude** region forbids cutting inside it.

When an operation has several regions, they are applied in feature-tree order:

1. If the first region is an include, the allowed area starts empty and the region is added to it.
2. If the first region is an exclude, the allowed area starts as everything the operation would
   cut, and the region is removed from it.
3. Each later include adds its area back; each later exclude removes its area again.

Several include regions together allow their combined area. With a mix of include and exclude
regions, the order decides the result, so keep the tree order readable and check the toolpath
after you reorder regions.

## Tool-centre boundary

The two modes treat the region's edge differently:

- **Include** means *everything inside must be cut*. To reach material right at the edge, such as
  a sharp corner, the cutter may reach past the region's boundary.
- **Exclude** means *the cutter stays out*. The whole cutter, not just its centre, is kept outside
  the region, with the operation's clearance.

Either way, a region can only reduce what an operation cuts. It never adds a cut that the
operation would not make without it. Entry moves, such as helices, and links between cuts also stay
inside the allowed area.

For V-carving, the groove gets narrower as it gets shallower, so with an include region the
shallower passes can reach slightly beyond the boundary. They never cut beyond what the operation
would cut without the region.

<Aside type="caution" title="An exclude region is not a clamp">
	Use clamps, not exclude regions, to keep the cutter away from workholding. Clamps are checked
	against the cutter's full width and their height, whatever the operation's strategy, and no
	region order can undo them. See [Clamps and clearances](/guide/cam-setup/clamps-and-clearances/).
</Aside>

## Where masks apply

| Operations | How the region is applied |
| --- | --- |
| Pocket, surface clean, V-carve offset, V-carve medial, and the 3D surface operations | Limits the area that is cleared or finished. |
| Edge route inside and outside, and engrave | Limits which parts of the path are cut. Edge routes honour regions only with the **Contour** strategy. |
| Drill | Limits which holes are drilled. |

While a region is applied, some options are replaced:

- **XY approach & exit** is not used. The operation reports **XY approach and exit are unavailable
  while a region mask is applied to this operation, so the ordinary entry and retract were used.**
- Check the operation's warnings after adding a region, and look at the toolpath: a masked result
  is not simply the unmasked toolpath with parts removed.
