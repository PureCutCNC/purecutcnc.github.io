A setup describes how the stock sits on the machine for a group of operations. Use Top and
Bottom setups when you need to turn the same part over and machine its other side.

## Main idea

A face is the side you draw and measure from. A setup holds the operations that machine that
side, together with its orientation and locating notes. Switching the view to an empty face
does not add a saved setup until you add content or save setup properties.

**Top** and **Bottom** are the two flat faces. **Around** exists only in **Rotary** projects
for work around bar stock.

## Choose a face

Open the current-face control beside the workspace tabs and choose **Top** or **Bottom**.
The control names the face in view. On Bottom, it includes the flip axis, for example
**Bottom · flipped X**. This compact label carries the flipped indication; it is not a
separate chip or banner row.

Bottom is shown as if you have turned the stock about the setup's flip axis. An X flip swaps
front and back; a Y flip swaps left and right. Draw and read Bottom features from that side.
Their **Z top** and **Z bottom** fields read with Bottom face up, while **Stock Z span** shows
the unchanged range in the stock.

When there is an opposite side to show, the menu offers **Show other side** with an eye icon.
Use it to show or hide the ghost overlay. Finish the current drawing or edit before switching
face; an unfinished edit keeps the other face unavailable.

## Features and the tree

New features belong to the face you draw them on. Other-face features that cannot be worked
from this side are shown faint and dashed when **Show other side** is on. They are references,
not selectable subjects for editing. Through subtracts and imported models are exceptions:
you can select them from either flat face for compatible operations.

In a two-sided project, the tree groups features under **Top face** and **Bottom face**.
Click a section heading to switch to that face. An empty face chosen from the menu has a
section reading **Nothing here yet** until you leave it. There are no ACTIVE or GHOST badges.
See [Feature tree and properties](/guide/fundamentals/feature-tree/#face-sections).

**Change authoring face…** changes which face owns a qualifying feature without moving its
volume in the stock. It is available only for features that span the whole stock, such as a
through hole or a full-height solid. The confirmation lists features that stay behind, including
locked features and those that do not qualify. Linked copies keep sharing their shape across
faces, so a later shape edit can affect copies on both sides.

## Through cuts and cross-face targets

A subtract whose Z range reaches both stock faces carries **THRU**. You can machine that
through-feature from either flat face. An imported 3D model can also be a target from either
side. A blind pocket on the other face is not a target from this side. A full-height add
feature stays a target of its own face, even though it is solid material you must machine
around from either side.

Select reachable features and use the Add button or **Add to operation** from their context
menu for a compatible operation. The operation's **Cuts** list shows its targets. A cross-face reach result uses ⇄
to mark a target reached from the other face.

When a through-feature has operations from both sides, **Check the passes meet** measures the
generated passes. It reports their reach in stock Z and whether they overlap or leave a gap.
Single-sided targets do not show this check. An unreachable target can still show an error.

<Aside type="caution" title="Meeting depths do not prove a complete cut">
The check measures generated tool motion at the target. It does not certify that every part of
the material has been removed or that the real cutter reaches it. Inspect both toolpaths and
verify tool length and workholding before cutting.
</Aside>

## Construction references across faces

Visible construction geometry on either flat face can guide work on the other:

| Reference | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| Point | Snap while drawing, moving, transforming, pasting, or measuring. |
| Path guide | Pick a guide for feature distribution or text layout. |
| Segment | Supply the reference for **Trim to cutting edge** or **Extend to target** while editing your own path. |

Canvas reference picks need **Show other side** on and the construction feature visible.
A visible construction row in the tree can supply an armed path-guide picker even with the
overlay hidden. Picking a reference does not select it for editing.

Other-face ordinary ghost features do not become construction references. You cannot trim,
extend, join, or transform a ghost as the subject of the command. Construction geometry cannot
be a machining target. Constraints do not use cross-face references, and dimensions anchored
across faces remain measurements rather than driving geometry.

See [Construction geometry](/guide/fundamentals/construction-geometry/#references-from-the-other-face)
for the reference workflow.

## Operations by setup

The CAM panel groups a two-sided job into setup cards. A header such as **Bottom 1 P02** means
one operation in the Bottom setup, program 02. An empty card reads **No operations**. Each card
has a gear for its setup properties and an export button when it has operations. A warning sign
calls attention to missing registration references.

The operations toolbar has **Add to Top** or **Add to Bottom**, **Plan**, **Export**, a
show/hide-all eye, and the generation gear. Add follows the face in view. Operations keep their
order within their setup. Selecting a dimmed operation from another setup switches the workspace
to its face so you can edit it.

To move an operation, use **Move…** beside **Setup** in its properties. Choose an existing
**Destination setup**, review **Targets removed by this move:**, then confirm **Move operation**.
Targets are checked from the destination face. A move is blocked if no valid targets would remain.

On Bottom, **Plan** proposes operations in the Bottom frame. A through-feature that Top
operations already cover fully is listed as **Covered by the Top setup.** See
[CAM Plan](/guide/cam-setup/cam-plan/#planning-the-bottom-setup).

## Setup properties and registration

Open a setup card's gear to edit its **Name** and **Operator notes**. With a single Top setup,
use **Setup properties** on the operations toolbar. **Face up** is a read-only
chip. The Top dialog keeps the shared touch-off note; Bottom also offers **Flip about** and
**Registration**.

For Bottom, choose **Stock X · front ↔ back** or **Stock Y · left ↔ right**. The sketch in the
dialog shows how the marked corner moves. All setups share one origin placement. Touch off
again after turning the stock.

Under **Registration**, choose a **Reference kind**: **Dowel**, **Fence**, or **Corner**.
Choose a **Reference target**: **Feature**, **Stock point**, or **Stock edge**, then use
**Add reference**. Coordinates describe the stock before it is turned. These entries record
your locating plan; the app does not verify that the stock is registered correctly.

Use **Save setup** to keep changes. **Delete setup…** sits at the bottom left. Its confirmation
lists the operations that will also be deleted; the last setup cannot be deleted.

<Aside type="danger" title="Safety: locate the part again after the flip">
A flip with the wrong axis, registration, or work zero can put the tool through the part or a
fixture. Check how the stock seats against your references and touch off at the stated location
before running the next program. Follow [Working safely](/guide/start-here/safety/).
</Aside>

## Preview, simulation, and output

The Sketch and 3D views show both setups' visible toolpaths in the same stock. The other setup
is faint beneath the active setup. A selected operation is drawn at full strength. On Bottom,
**Isometric view** looks from below, where the Bottom paths approach the stock.

Simulation follows the workspace face control and runs one setup at a time. With several
setups, the playback toolbar's information button explains that the run starts on fresh stock:
material removed by another setup is not shown. See
[Simulation](/guide/verify-export/simulation/#simulating-a-setup).

A Top/Bottom export writes one numbered program for each setup with selected operations. Each
program carries setup instructions, including orientation, touch-off, registration, and notes.
A single Top setup keeps its existing output and filename convention. Booklets name the
operation's setup and program and record registration, so you can match the paperwork to the
program. See [Exporting G-code](/guide/verify-export/gcode-export/#one-program-per-setup) and
[Setup booklets](/guide/verify-export/setup-booklets/#setup-and-program-identity).

## Typical two-sided job

1. Create a milling project and set the stock and origin.
2. On Top, draw the features and add the operations for that side.
3. Choose Bottom, draw the Bottom features, and add its operations.
4. Open Bottom setup properties. Choose the flip axis and record your locating references and notes.
5. Inspect both setups' paths. Check any through-feature cut from both sides, then simulate each setup separately.
6. Export and review the numbered programs and booklets. At the machine, verify each setup, locate the stock, and touch off before its program.
