Use the creation drawer's rotary group to describe a turned diameter, flats, a thread, or flutes.
These are editable canned features: their parameters stay with the shape. Creating one does not
create a machining operation or ask for a tool.

## Before you start

- Create a **Rotary** project and set the bar's shape, size, length, and axis.
- Check the chuck and tailstock. See [Rotary machining](/guide/rotary/overview/).
- For **Thread** or **Flutes**, use a round bar and choose **Around**. **Turn** and
  **Polygon / hex** also work from Top and Bottom, on round or square stock.

## Create a rotary feature

<Steps>

1. Open the shape drawer in the creation rail. Its rotary group contains **Thread**, **Flutes**,
   **Turn**, and **Polygon / hex**.

2. Choose a feature and enter its fields in the canvas side panel, as described below.

3. Select **Create**. Check the feature in the tree and in the **3D** view.

4. Add its machining operations through CAM, or review **Plan** on Around. See
   [Rotary operations](/guide/rotary/rotary-operations/).

</Steps>

The group stays visible when an entry cannot be used. The reason can be **Needs a bar in a
rotary project**, **Only available on the around face**, or **Turn a section of the square bar
round first**. On a square blank, Thread and Flutes need a section that a Turn has made round;
see [Sheet work on a square bar](#sheet-work-on-a-square-bar). Turn and Polygon are always
available.

## Turn a diameter

**Turn** opens **Turn a diameter** and creates one generated model. It describes the shape to
keep; add rotary rough and finish operations separately.

| Field | Meaning and initial value |
| --- | --- |
| **Start from chuck end (mm)** / **Start from chuck end (in)** | Distance along the bar; starts at 0. |
| **Length (mm)** / **Length (in)** | Length of the turned section; starts at the full bar length. |
| **Diameter (mm)** / **Diameter (in)** | Diameter to keep; starts at the round diameter or square side. It cannot exceed that stock size. |
| **Profile: pick a shape** | Starts at **None — one plain diameter**. Pick a drawn outline to define a shaped Turn instead. |
| **Refresh** | Recopy the picked source outline after editing it. Appears only after a profile is picked. |

Start and length must fit inside the bar. A full-length default does not mean the cutter can
reach through the chuck: the operations still respect workholding and end supports.

To use a profile, draw a half profile or one closed outline on **Top**, inside the bar and on
one side of the centreline. Pick it in **Profile: pick a shape**. The outline is revolved about
the bar axis. A closed outline away from the axis can describe a ring or groove. The profile
sets start, length, and diameter; those fields are not separate overrides while it is picked.

The source must have one outline, must not cross the axis or reach outside the bar, and must
not double back along it. A pick copies the outline. Later edits to the source do not silently
change the Turn: reopen the Turn and use **Refresh**. If the source is deleted, the copied
profile is kept.

## Cut a polygon or hexagon

**Polygon / hex** opens **Cut a polygon** and creates one generated model. Its fields are:

| Field | Meaning and initial value |
| --- | --- |
| **Start from chuck end (mm)** / **Start from chuck end (in)** | Starts at 0. |
| **Length (mm)** / **Length (in)** | Starts at the full bar length; start and length must fit inside it. |
| **Sides** | Starts at 6 for a hexagon. Use a whole number from 3 to 64. |
| **Across flats (mm)** / **Across flats (in)** | Distance between flats; starts at three quarters of the diameter or square side. The model must fit inside the stock. |
| **Rotation (°)** | Orientation about the bar axis; starts at 0. |

The panel notes **Flats are approximated on the around view.** Inspect the model and generated
motion in 3D before choosing the cutter and stepover.

## Cut a thread

**Thread** opens **Cut a thread**. It creates one canned line feature, with evenly spaced
starts when you ask for more than one. Each start advances by starts × pitch per full turn.

| Field | Meaning and initial value |
| --- | --- |
| **Start from chuck end (mm)** / **Start from chuck end (in)** | Starts at 0. |
| **Length (mm)** / **Length (in)** | Starts at the full bar length. |
| **Pitch (mm)** | Spacing between adjacent threads in a metric project; starts at 1.5 mm. |
| **Threads per inch** | Replaces pitch in an inch project; starts at 20. |
| **Starts** | Number of equally spaced thread starts, from 1 to 32; starts at 1. |
| **Depth (mm)** / **Depth (in)** | Radial depth; initially 0.61 × pitch: 0.915 mm or 0.0305 in at the default pitch. |
| **Direction** | **Right-hand** by default, or **Left-hand**. |

While creating a thread, changing pitch updates its default depth until you edit depth yourself.
An existing thread keeps its chosen depth when you edit pitch. Depth must stay above the bar
centreline. Add an **Engrave** operation with a suitable V-bit, or use CAM Plan on Around.

<Aside type="caution" title="A radial V-bit thread is an approximation">
The panel's notice describes an approximation to a 60° thread at small helix angles, for wood,
plastic, and decorative work. Do not use it as a claim of precision thread tooling or fit.
</Aside>

## Cut flutes

**Flutes** opens **Cut flutes** and creates one canned line feature.

| Field | Meaning and initial value |
| --- | --- |
| **Start from chuck end (mm)** / **Start from chuck end (in)** | Starts at 0. |
| **Length (mm)** / **Length (in)** | Starts at the full bar length. |
| **Flutes** | Number equally spaced around the bar, from 1 to 64; starts at 4. |
| **Twist (turns)** | Total turns along the length; starts at 0 for straight flutes. Enter a nonnegative magnitude. |
| **Direction** | **Right-hand** by default, or **Left-hand**; chooses the hand of a nonzero twist. |
| **Depth (mm)** / **Depth (in)** | Starts at one quarter of the starting surface radius. It must remain above the centreline. |

Choose the cutter on the machining operation, not in this panel. CAM Plan proposes an
**Engrave** with a V-bit for Flutes as well as Thread; review whether that cutter makes the
flute shape you want.

## Edit and follow a Turn

Select the feature and use **Edit turn…**, **Edit polygon…**, **Edit thread…**, or **Edit flutes…** in Properties to reopen its parameters. The panel is
**Edit turn**, **Edit polygon**, **Edit thread**, or **Edit flutes**. Use **Save** to keep changes
or **Cancel** to discard them. Parameters own the rotary feature's Z span; change diameter,
across flats, or depth in this panel rather than looking for an ordinary Z slider.

A Thread or Flutes feature wholly over a single plain-diameter section of a Turn measures its
depth below that Turn's surface. Only Turns and models **above it in the feature tree** count:
a Thread above its Turn in the tree measures from the bar surface, and on a plain shank it can
cut nothing. Drag the Thread below the Turn to follow it. The panel names the Turn and diameter. Its surface height
follows changes to that Turn. A pattern moved with its Turn keeps that relationship where
it still lies over the same section; check its start and length after moving or resizing either.
Your pitch and depth choices remain yours.

A pattern crossing a Turn end, a diameter step, a taper, or another model can have no single
Turn diameter to follow. The panel warns and measures depth from the bar surface instead.
Read that notice and inspect the resulting cut before adding or regenerating operations.

## Drawing on Around

On a round bar's **Around** sheet, use the ordinary drawing tools, text, or imported 2D artwork.
Their depth is measured into the bar; a cut must stop short of its centreline. A newly drawn
Around shape starts at one sixteenth of the diameter deep, rounded to 0.5 mm or 1/64 in and
capped at one quarter of the diameter. Check its Z fields before machining.

The sheet edges are the same seam, labelled −180° and +180°. Canned outlines and toolpaths
that cross an edge are displayed wrapped back into the sheet. A many-turn thread therefore
looks like a set of stripes. The geometry and machining path stay continuous; hand-drawn
outlines remain displayed where you drew them.

Named bands show the length occupied by models inside the bar. You can select a model band
for rotary operations. It represents the model rather than a pocket drawn on the sheet.

### Work on a Turn

The sheet is drawn at the bar's own size, but work on a Turn is cut on a smaller surface. When
all of an operation's targets lie on one Turn diameter above them in the tree, the operation is
planned at that Turn's radius across the bar, on a round or a square bar. V-carved text then
keeps square stroke ends, and pocket walls sit one cutter radius in. Where the artwork lands and
its depths do not change. Drilling, and work that is not all on one Turn diameter, keep the
bar's sheet. In the 3D view, a pocket whose top sits on the Turn's surface shows open.

### Starting at the Turn's surface

Around depths are measured from the bar surface, also over a Turn. A pocket, island, line,
text, or canned flat shape whose top is above the one Turn it lies on would cut air on its first
pass. Its tree row shows ⚠, and Properties says, for example, **Starts 2.5 mm above the surface
of Turn 1: the first pass cuts air.** Choose **Start at turn surface** to lower its Z top and Z
bottom by that amount, in one undo step. A shape that crosses a Turn's end or a diameter
change gets a note that there is no single surface to start from, and no button. Thread and
Flutes are left out: they follow their Turn by themselves.

## Sheet work on a square bar

On a square blank, Around sheet work (pockets, islands, lines, text, canned flat shapes, Thread
and Flutes) is cut only over a section that a Turn **above it in the feature tree** has made
round. Until any section is round, the drawing tools and the Add menu say **Turn a section of
the square bar round first**. Region and construction drawing stay available.

<Steps>

1. Create a Turn whose diameter fits inside the square, at the length you want to work on.
2. Draw the sheet work below that Turn in the feature tree, over its length.
3. Add its operations on Around as you would on a round bar.

</Steps>

A section does not count when the Turn is wider than the square's inscribed circle, or when a
Polygon or an imported model is above the feature instead: those leave flats. Work that is not
over a turned section is kept, with a note such as **Kept, but it cannot be cut: turn this
section round first.** Moving the Turn below it in the tree has the same effect. An operation
is judged by its cutter's reach along the bar too, so a route whose path runs past the Turn's
end is refused even when the feature is over the Turn. One operation cuts under one Turn: split
targets that sit under different Turns into one operation per Turn.

Between passes the program travels above the square's corners. See
[Square bar sheet work](/guide/rotary/rotary-operations/#square-bar-sheet-work).
