Rotary work turns a bar under a milling cutter. You can draw on its unrolled surface or machine
a model inside it. This is wrapped 4th-axis milling, not lathe turning: the cutter is in the
spindle and the rotary axis positions the stock.

## Main idea

Choose **Rotary** under **Project type** when you create the project. The type is read-only
afterwards. See [Project type](/guide/fundamentals/projects-and-files/#project-type).
A rotary project can use **Top**, **Bottom**, and **Around** on the same bar. It does not provide
a general simultaneous 5-axis workflow.

## Bar stock

Select **Stock** in the tree and check these fields:

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Shape** | **Round bar** or **Square blank**. You can switch between them within a rotary project. |
| **Diameter** | The round bar's measured diameter. |
| **Side** | The square blank's measured side, across its flats. |
| **Length** | The full length along the bar, including material held at the ends. |
| **Axis** | **X** or **Y**, matching the direction the bar lies on the machine. |

The initial origin is at the chuck end, on the centreline in plan and on top of the bar.
Check it against your touch-off plan. The bar's centreline is halfway up its diameter or side;
Z 0 in the project is at the bottom of its bounding box.

## Chuck and tailstock

Bar stock has a **Chuck** clamp. Use a clamp's **Type** to choose **Clamp**, **Chuck**, or
**Tailstock**. Chuck and tailstock keep-outs stay centred on the bar; their **Diameter** replaces
the ordinary clamp Z range. Enter the real workholding dimensions and position.

The chuck adds **Chuck size**, **Minimum grip diameter**, **Maximum grip diameter**, and
**Jaws**. The listed sizes are **3"**, **4"**, **5"**, **6"**, **8"**, **10"**, and **12"**.
They are equipment presets, not a guarantee of your chuck's capacity. The starter is the
4-inch size when it fits; otherwise the app chooses a fitting listed size. Check the limits
against your actual chuck and jaw set, then edit them if needed. A size or capacity you choose
stays your equipment choice when the bar changes.

**Jaws** offers **3**, **4**, or **6**. Round stock starts with 3 jaws; square stock starts with 4.
For a square blank, 4 jaws compare the grip range with the side across flats; 3 or 6 compare it
with the circle through the corners. An unknown capacity reads **Capacity unknown. Enter limits
or pick a size.** A bar outside the chuck's range shows **Bar is outside the chuck’s clamping
range.** If the bar fits none of the listed ranges, do not treat the default body as a valid grip.

<Aside type="danger" title="Safety: keep the bar held and supported">
A loose bar or a cutter striking the chuck or tailstock can damage the machine and throw material.
Check the real grip, end support, rotating corner clearance, and cutter reach. Leave enough
material to support the part throughout the job; see [Working safely](/guide/start-here/safety/).
</Aside>

## Machine rotary axis

The built-in rotary machines, such as **grblHAL + rotary A** or **GRBL 1.1 + rotary (borrowed
motor)**, take their rotary orientation from the stock: the rotary turns about **Stock** >
**Axis**, so one machine serves a bar along X or along Y and there is nothing to set. The machine
list shows **Follows the bar (now along X)**, and export says the A orientation follows the stock.
Two of them need a project in mm. See
[Built-in rotary machines](/guide/cam-setup/machines/#built-in-rotary-machines).

A machine of your own has a fixed orientation. In the machine editor, set **Rotary axis** to
**Bar along X** or **Bar along Y** to match **Stock** > **Axis**. **None** is the default. Under
**Driven by**, choose **Its own axis (A)**, or **The Y motor** for a bar along X / **The X motor**
for a bar along Y. A borrowed motor cannot also provide linear travel during that program. Saving
or duplicating a built-in rotary machine gives the copy the open project's bar axis, fixed; change
it in the editor if you later turn a bar along the other axis.

The **Program word: A** note shows the output letter; it changes to the borrowed motor's
letter when applicable. **Reverse direction** reverses the rotary sign. Confirm the direction
on your controller before cutting. You can design on Around before choosing a suitable machine,
but export checks the machine. See [Machines](/guide/cam-setup/machines/#rotary-axis).

## Around and the unrolled sheet

Open the current-face menu beside the workspace tabs and choose **Around**. The Sketch view
lays out the bar surface flat: length along the bar by one circumference around it. A line marks
each full turn. The solid zero line, A = 0, is in the middle, on the bar's centreline in plan.
The origin marker sits on that line. Negative A is below the zero line in the unrolled view.
The sheet's opposite edges meet at the back of the bar, labelled −180° and +180°.

A distance of one circumference around the sheet becomes one full rotation. Geometry can run
past one turn. Canned features and toolpaths are displayed folded into the sheet; this display
does not split the cut into separate features. Named model bands show where models sit along
the bar. See [Rotary features](/guide/rotary/rotary-features/#drawing-on-around).

## Top and Bottom on a bar

Top and Bottom machine the bar as material too. A pocket does not need a base add feature.
Bottom flips about the bar's own axis. Top programs use A = 0°; Bottom programs use A = 180°.
With a rotary machine, the program retracts to the machine safe Z before the opening turn.
A machine without a rotary axis records the angle as an operator comment instead.
See [Setups and faces](/guide/fundamentals/setups-and-faces/) and
[Rotary export](/guide/rotary/rotary-operations/#rotary-export).

<Screenshot src={topBottom} maxWidth={775} alt="3D view of a Top pocket toolpath on a 40 mm square blank" caption="Top and Bottom machine a flat face of the bar." />

## The bar is the material

On Top, Bottom, and Around, a shape drawn on its own starts as **Subtract**: a cut into the bar.
Inside a cut, it starts as **Add**: an island left standing. Inside that island, it is
**Subtract** again. Check the role after drawing, especially across the sheet seam: nesting
across opposite seam edges is not inferred. You can change **Operation** in the shape properties.

## What Around does not offer

Around is a surface wrapped about the bar, rather than another flat side:

- Tabs are not used there. Keep the part attached through uncut bar and end supports.
- **Show other side**, flat-face authoring-face changes, and flat-face cross-face targets do not
  apply to the sheet. Use the face where the feature was drawn; rotary models have their own targets.
- The flat **3D surface rough**, **3D surface finish**, and **3D surface cleanup** operations have
  no wrapped form. Use **Rotary rough** and **Rotary finish** for models inside a bar.
- Printing and 2D design export use flat faces. Switch to Top or Bottom for those outputs.

On a square blank, sheet work such as pockets, text, or Thread is cut only over a section a
Turn above it in the feature tree has made round. Turn the section first, then draw on it. See
[Sheet work on a square bar](/guide/rotary/rotary-features/#sheet-work-on-a-square-bar).

## Zero A before each job

Every Around operation ends by turning A forward to the next whole turn, so the bar finishes at
0 up. A Top or Bottom bar program does the same after its last operation. Before each job, put the bar's intended zero line up and zero A. Between two rotary
operations the program either runs the machine's **Zero A line** or pauses for you to zero A by
hand; see [Rotary export](/guide/rotary/rotary-operations/#rotary-export). When a motor is
borrowed, use the corresponding controller axis and output letter.

<Aside type="danger" title="Safety: check the rotary zero before starting">
A wrong rotary zero puts every wrapped cut at the wrong angle. Confirm the bar orientation,
rotary zero, XYZ touch-off, direction, and first turn before running the program. Follow
[Working safely](/guide/start-here/safety/).
</Aside>
