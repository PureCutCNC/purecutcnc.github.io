This walkthrough takes a blank project to exported G-code: a rectangular part with a circular
pocket, cut with a 1/4″ endmill. It takes about ten minutes. You need PureCut CNC open in a
desktop or tablet browser, or the desktop app; see
[Install and open PureCut CNC](/guide/start-here/install-and-open/).

The screenshots on this page come from an earlier version of the app, so some labels and panels
differ from what you see. The steps describe the app as it is.

## How it works

The Quick Start explains only what its steps need. Three ideas carry it:

- Every shape you draw is a feature. Its role, add or subtract, says whether it is material or
  a cavity, and its **Z top** and **Z bottom** say where it starts and ends vertically.
- An operation turns selected features into a toolpath, using a tool from the project's tool
  list.
- The stock, origin, units, tools, machine, and operations are all saved in the project file.

[Features, roles, and depth](/guide/fundamentals/features-and-roles/) and
[Stock, origin, and units](/guide/fundamentals/stock-origin-units/) explain these in full.

## Create a new project

Click **New project** in the top toolbar. In the **New project** dialog, select the **Blank
imperial** template ("Empty project in inches."). The **Template preview** lists inch units, a
4 × 3 × 0.75 stock, and no machine. Click **Create project**.

The workspace opens in **Sketch** with an empty canvas. The origin sits at the lower-left corner of
the stock, on its top surface.

<Screenshot src={newProject} alt="New project dialog with the Blank imperial template selected and a preview listing inch units, a 4 × 3 × 0.75 stock, and no machine" />

## Add the outer boundary rectangle

In the tool rail, choose the creation target that makes features (its tooltip reads **Create
features**), then choose **Rectangle** in the shape drawer. When the workflow panel asks you to
**Click first corner**, click about 0.5 in inside the top-left corner of the stock. At **Click
opposite corner or enter dimensions**, click **Dimensions** and enter a **Width** of 3 and a
**Height** of 2.

<Screenshot src={newRect} alt="Sketch view with a pending 3 × 2 rectangle inside the stock outline, and the Rectangle workflow panel asking for the opposite corner or dimensions" />

The rectangle appears in the feature tree. As the first solid in the project it is always an add
feature: the material that remains. The stock around it is what an outside edge route would cut
away later.

## Add a circle inside the rectangle

Choose **Circle**. At **Click center point**, click the centre of the rectangle. At **Click to set
radius or enter dimensions**, click **Dimensions** and enter a **Radius** of 0.75.

<Screenshot src={newCircle} alt="Sketch view with a circle being drawn inside a rectangle at radius 0.75, and the Circle workflow panel offering Dimensions and Cancel" />

Because the circle lies inside an add feature, PureCut CNC makes it a subtract feature. Select it
and check its properties: **Operation** reads **Subtract**, and **Z top** is 0.75, the top of the
stock. Set **Z bottom** to 0.5. The pocket floor is then 0.5 in above the bottom of the stock, so
the pocket is 0.25 in deep.

<Aside title="Z is a coordinate, not a depth">
	**Z bottom** is where the pocket floor is, not how deep to cut. The depth is **Z top** minus
	**Z bottom**: 0.75 − 0.5 = 0.25 in.
</Aside>

## Import a 1/4″ endmill from the tool library

Open the **Tools** tab in the CAM panel and click **Import from library…**. The **Import tools
from library** dialog lists 34 bundled metric and imperial tools. Type `1/4` in the search box,
tick **1/4" Endmill**, and click **Import tool**. Tools already in the project are marked **In
project**, so you do not import them twice.

<Screenshot src={importTool} alt="Empty Tools list in the CAM panel with the Add tool and Import from library buttons" maxWidth={320} />

Check that the tool's diameter and type match the cutter you will put in the spindle. Library
values are a starting point, not a measurement of your tool. The
[Tool library](/guide/cam-setup/tool-library/) page covers the dialog in detail.

## Create a rough pocket operation

Select the circle on the canvas or in the feature tree. Open the **Operations** tab, click **Add**,
and on the **Pocket** row click **Rough**. The Add menu lists the operations your selection can
use first. **Finish** would add a finishing pass instead, and **Both** adds one of each.

<Screenshot src={addPocketRough} alt="Add menu open on the Pocket row, with Rough, Finish, and Both buttons above the Pocket description and its key points" maxWidth={420} />

The new **Pocket Rough** operation appears in the operations list. Select it to see its settings.
PureCut CNC chooses a suitable tool from the project; check that **Tool** shows **1/4" Endmill**.
Then look at **Stepdown**, **Stepover ratio**, **Feed**, **Plunge feed**, and **RPM**. They start
from the tool's defaults, which are not a recommendation for your material or machine. For this
walkthrough you can leave them as they are.

A new operation's toolpath is generated and shown straight away. The eye button on the operation's
row hides and shows it.

<Aside type="tip" title="Optional: spiral into the cut instead of plunging">
	In the operation's **Entry & retract** group, set **Entry strategy** to **Helix**. The tool
	then spirals down into each level instead of dropping straight in, which is easier on the
	cutter and easy to see in the 3D view. See
	[Entry and exit moves](/guide/strategies/entry-and-exit/).
</Aside>

## Preview the toolpath

Switch between the **Sketch**, **3D view**, and **Simulation** tabs above the canvas at any time.

- **Sketch** draws the toolpath over the features. You can still change any feature or operation
  here.
- **3D view** draws the toolpath over the stock and the model. Drag to orbit and scroll to zoom
  at the pointer; the camera menu has the standard views.
- **Simulation** removes material from the stock along the toolpath. In **Selected** mode, with
  the operation selected, **Play** runs the tool through the cut.

The toolpath display controls choose which moves are drawn: **Cuts**, **Lead-ins**, **Rapids**,
**Plunges**, **Retractions**, and **Directions**. Turn off the cuts, for example, to look at just
the entry moves.

<Screenshot src={previewSketch} alt="Sketch view with the pocket toolpath drawn as concentric rings inside the circle, over the rectangle" />

<Screenshot src={preview3d} alt="3D view of the stock and the raised rectangle, with the circular pocket's toolpath drawn as concentric rings" />

<Screenshot src={previewSimulation} alt="Simulation view showing the stock after the pocket toolpath has removed material" />

Use the preview to answer specific questions. Does the pocket stop at the floor you set? Does the
toolpath stay inside the circle? Does the tool enter and leave where you expect? If anything looks
wrong, go back to **Sketch**, change the feature or operation, and look again. The toolpath is
generated again after each change.

## Select a machine and export G-code

A new project has no machine. Select **Project** at the top of the feature tree and set
**Machine** to the definition for your controller, for example **Mach3**. The machine sets the
G-code dialect, the units commands, and the file extension.

In the CAM panel, click **Export**. The **Export G-code** dialog shows:

- **Machine**, with **Change** to choose a different one;
- **Origin**: export uses the current project origin as machine X0 Y0 Z0;
- **Project units**;
- **Operations**: the operations to export. An operation that is off or has no tool cannot be
  ticked. **Pocket Rough** starts ticked because its toolpath is shown;
- **Options**, any **Errors** and **Warnings**, and **Preview (first 30 lines)** of the program.

Check each of them, then click the export button (**Export .tap** for Mach3) and save the file.
Open the file in your controller or a G-code viewer and compare it with your setup.

<Aside type="danger" title="Safety: verify before cutting">
	A preview and an exported file do not prove a job is safe. Check the program in your
	controller, confirm that the work zero matches the project origin, and run the job with the
	spindle off and the tool above the stock before cutting material. See
	[Working safely](/guide/start-here/safety/).
</Aside>

## Your turn

You have a project with a rough pocket in a rectangular part. Each of these builds on it:

1. **Leave material for a finish pass.** In **Pocket Rough**, set **Stock to leave radial** and
   **Stock to leave axial** so a later pass has something to clean up.
2. **Add a finish pass.** Select the circle again and add **Pocket** > **Finish**.
3. **Rough the outside.** Select the rectangle and add **Edge route outside** > **Rough**.
4. **Finish the outside.** Add **Edge route outside** > **Finish** on the same rectangle.
5. **Hold the part with tabs.** Select the outside route and click **Auto place tabs** in its
   settings. See [Tabs](/guide/cam-setup/tabs/).
6. **Add clamps.** Under **Clamps** in the feature tree, use **Add clamp** to place a clamp on each
   side of the stock, then check that the toolpaths clear them. See
   [Clamps and clearances](/guide/cam-setup/clamps-and-clearances/).
7. **Try trochoidal roughing.** In the outside rough route, set **Strategy** to **Trochoidal**.
   See [Trochoidal cutting](/guide/strategies/trochoidal-cutting/).
8. **Relieve square corners.** Draw a square pocket instead of the circle, add a pocket, and set
   **Corner relief** to **Dogbone** so a square-cornered part fits into it. See
   [Corner rounding and relief](/guide/strategies/corners/).
